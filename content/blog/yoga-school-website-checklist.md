A student who is planning a 200-hour teacher training or a week-long retreat is making a big decision. They are choosing where to travel, how much to spend and who to trust. Before they message you, they will almost always open your website. If it answers their questions clearly, they enquire. If it leaves them guessing, they open the next school's website.

This checklist is written for yoga school, teacher training and retreat owners. It covers what your website needs so that a visitor, even from another country, can understand your offer, trust you and book.

## The short version

A good yoga school website does five things: it shows exactly what you offer, it makes dates and prices easy to find, it proves you are real and experienced, it makes enquiry and payment simple, and it loads fast on any phone.

## Part 1: Clarity and trust

### 1. A homepage that says who you are in five seconds
Say what you teach, where you are and who it is for. For example: "200-hour yoga teacher training in Rishikesh, for beginners and practising students." Put your main button ("View dates" or "Apply now") at the top.

### 2. A separate page for every course and retreat
Give each course its own page, such as 200-hour YTT, 300-hour YTT, a 7-day retreat or an online programme. People search for specific things, and a dedicated page can show up in Google for that specific search. Each page should cover the curriculum, daily schedule, who it is for, what is included and how to apply.

### 3. Dates, prices and inclusions, up front
Hiding prices makes visitors suspicious and creates extra back-and-forth. Show start dates, the price, what is included (meals, stay, materials) and what is not (flights, visa, insurance). Clear information filters in the right students and saves your time.

### 4. Real teacher profiles
Add each teacher's name, photo, training background, years of teaching and teaching style. Mention certifications and registrations only if they are genuinely yours, and keep the wording accurate. Never use titles or claims you cannot support.

### 5. Practical details for travellers
Explain the location, how to reach you (nearest airport or station), the accommodation, food (vegetarian, vegan, allergies), the daily timetable, weather by season and any visa or travel notes. International students always look for this.

## Part 2: Booking and communication

### 6. A simple application or enquiry form
Ask for the basics: name, email, country, course, preferred dates and a short message. Every extra field reduces the number of people who finish it. Make sure the form actually reaches you, and test it regularly.

### 7. Clear payment and refund terms
Explain how to reserve a spot (for example, a deposit), which payment methods and currencies you accept, and what your cancellation and refund policy is. Put it in plain words on a dedicated page. Clear terms prevent disputes later and build trust before the booking.

### 8. Easy ways to contact you
Add WhatsApp, email and, if you wish, a booking call. Mention your time zone and when you usually reply, so someone writing from another continent knows what to expect.

## Part 3: Proof and content

### 9. Real photos and a short video
Show your actual shala, rooms, food and teachers. A one-minute video of a class or a tour of the space helps people picture themselves there. Stock photos make every school look the same.

### 10. Genuine reviews
Link to your real Google, TripAdvisor or other review pages, or show testimonials with the student's permission. Never publish fake or invented reviews. Students can usually tell, and platforms can penalise them.

### 11. An honest FAQ
Answer the questions every student asks: Do I need prior experience? What does a typical day look like? How many students are in a batch? What happens if I cannot attend? What should I bring? A good FAQ replaces many emails.

## Part 4: Technical basics

### 12. Fast and easy on mobile
Many visitors will open your site on a phone, sometimes on a slow connection. Compress images, avoid heavy sliders and autoplay videos, and make buttons easy to tap.

### 13. Built for visitors from other countries
Use simple, clear English (or add the languages your students speak). Show prices in a currency people understand, mention time zones for online sessions and avoid slang that does not translate.

### 14. Basic SEO and a Google Business Profile
Give each page its own title and description, one main heading and clean URLs. If you have a physical location, set up and verify your Google Business Profile so you appear when people search nearby or look for your school by name. Submit your site to Google Search Console.

### 15. A site you own, plus an email list
Social media and booking marketplaces are useful, but algorithms and rules change. Make sure your own website is the centre: you own the domain, you control the content and you collect enquiries and emails there. A simple email list lets you reach interested students again for the next batch.

## Privacy and legal basics

Add a privacy policy and, where your visitors' country requires it, a cookie notice. Check the rules that apply to you and your students, as they differ between countries.

## FAQ

**How many pages does a yoga school website need?**
A homepage, one page for each course or retreat, a teachers page, an accommodation and travel page, a FAQ, a contact or apply page and your policies. For many schools this is around 8 to 15 pages.

**Do I need online payment on my website?**
Not always at the start. Many schools begin with an enquiry form and a deposit link, then add full online booking when they are ready.

**Should I list my prices on the website?**
In most cases, yes. Clear prices build trust and reduce unsuitable enquiries.

**Can a website help students from other countries find my school?**
Yes. A clear, fast site with a page for each course helps people find you through search, and gives your social media followers a place to check details and enquire.
